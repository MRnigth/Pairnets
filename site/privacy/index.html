Privacy Policy

What Pairnets knows about you, why, and for how long. As little as it can, and never your files.

Last updated 10 October 2026

The short version

  • Your files are stored only on your nest, your own server. They pass through sync.pairnets.app on the way and are never kept there.
  • Your Pairnets account stores your email address, how you sign in, and which computers and nests you have linked.
  • No ads, no tracking, no analytics. We never sell your data or share it for marketing.
  • This website, pairnets.app, sets no cookies and collects nothing.
  • You can see most of it on your account page, and delete all of it with one button.

1. Who is responsible

Pairnets is made and run by MRnigth, a private person in Denmark. MRnigth is the data controller for the Pairnets website and the Pairnets account service. In this policy, “we” and “us” mean MRnigth.

Questions or requests about your data: support@pairnets.app.

2. What this policy covers

  • The website pairnets.app.
  • The account service sync.pairnets.app, including its API and the relay that connects your computers to your nest.
  • The Pairnets apps for Windows, Mac and Linux.
  • The nest software (the Pairnets server), as far as it sends anything to us.

It doesn't cover your nest itself. That's your own server, and you decide what happens on it (see Your nest). It also doesn't cover other websites we link to, such as GitHub or Google.

4. Your files

  • Your files are stored on your nest and on the computers you sync. Nowhere else.
  • When a computer reaches your nest through sync.pairnets.app, the files travel through the service and through Cloudflare's network on the way. Both legs use HTTPS, but HTTPS is unwrapped at Cloudflare while the traffic is passed on, as with any Cloudflare Tunnel. The service streams it straight through: it never stores file names or contents, and never writes them to a log.
  • Pairnets doesn't encrypt files end to end yet.
  • We don't look at your files, scan them, sell them, or use them to train anything.

5. The apps

  • No telemetry, no analytics, no crash reports sent anywhere.
  • The apps talk to your nest (directly, or through sync.pairnets.app), to sync.pairnets.app to sign in (sending the computer's name, its system and the Pairnets version), and to GitHub to check for updates at start-up and once a day. GitHub sees your IP address when the app checks. You can turn the check off in Settings → Updates and speed.
  • On your computer, the app keeps its settings, the computer's key (encrypted by Windows, or kept in the Mac Keychain or the Linux keyring), your account's email address to show you which account you're using, and a log. Report a bug saves the report on your computer and sends nothing.
  • Settings → Start over → Reset this app removes all of it.

6. Your nest

Your nest is your own server. You decide what it keeps and who gets in, and you are responsible for the data on it. We can't see it.

  • The nest software keeps your files and their old versions (for 30 days), the list of computers (name, system, Pairnets version, when last seen), sign-in sessions for its website, and logs. The logs include IP addresses of failed sign-ins and of new computers.
  • A nest linked to your Pairnets account gets its requests through sync.pairnets.app. When you allow a computer, the service tells your nest the computer's name and a masked form of your email address (like m***@example.com), so the nest can show who allowed it.
  • The one-line installer sends the server's name and Pairnets version to sync.pairnets.app, to link the nest to your account.
  • If you let other people use your nest, you are responsible for their data on it.

7. This website (pairnets.app)

  • Plain pages: no cookies, no analytics, no tracking, no scripts or fonts from other sites. The only thing it keeps is a note in your browser that you closed the cookie notice (pn-cookie-notice, never sent anywhere). Nothing else.
  • Cloudflare Pages delivers the site. Cloudflare handles your IP address and the pages you ask for, to deliver and protect it.
  • Download links go to GitHub, where GitHub's privacy statement applies.

9. Why we may use your data

Under the GDPR we need a legal basis for each use:

  • To give you what you asked for: your account, signing in, linking computers and nests, and passing traffic to your nest. Basis: our agreement with you (Article 6(1)(b)).
  • To keep Pairnets safe: limits on requests, the bot check, the activity log and technical logs. Basis: our legitimate interest in protecting your account and the service from abuse (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • To follow the law, where a law requires it (Article 6(1)(c)).

We don't make automated decisions about you, and we don't build profiles.

11. Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask to see the data we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, to get a copy in a common format, and to restrict or object to how we use it.

  • Most of it is on your account page at sync.pairnets.app/account, and Delete my account there removes all of it. See Delete my account.
  • For anything else, email support@pairnets.app from the address on your account. It's free, and we answer within one month.
  • You can complain to the Danish Data Protection Agency, Datatilsynet, or to the data protection authority where you live.

12. Security

We use HTTPS everywhere. Sign-in links, keys and sessions are stored only in hashed form. Cookies can only be read by our own site. Every computer has its own key, which you can remove at any time. The service doesn't store your files.

It isn't perfect. There's no end-to-end encryption yet, so Cloudflare sees traffic in transit. Anyone who can sign in to your Pairnets account, and the service itself, can allow a new computer to reach your nest. Protect your email and Google account. More on the Security page.

13. Children

Pairnets isn't for children under 13. If you think a child under 13 has an account, email us and we'll delete it.

14. Changes

If we change this policy, we update the date at the top. If a change matters, we tell you by email or on the sign-in page before it takes effect.
